Update /skills/enterprise-readiness/SKILL.md with the following additional principles.

11. Do not manufacture precision.

Do not invent decision dates, delivery windows, review durations, effort estimates, financial exposure, or deadlines when the source information does not support them.

When timing matters but is unknown:
- state that the timing is unknown
- identify who must provide it
- explain why it matters

12. Before declaring an outcome impossible, test whether any unresolved assumption, mitigation, acceleration, scope change, interim control, or alternative path could change the outcome.

If such a path has not been evaluated, classify the outcome as at risk and identify the question that must be resolved.

"Will fail" or "cannot be achieved" should be reserved for situations where the evidence logically forecloses all viable paths.

13. Optimize the primary output for executive consumption.

The main weekly readiness review should answer, in this order:

A. What needs attention?
B. Why does it matter?
C. What decision is required?
D. Who owns that decision?
E. What happens if we do nothing?

Keep detailed evidence, assumptions, dependency analysis, and missing information available as supporting analysis rather than putting everything in the executive view.

14. Prefer signal over completeness.

Do not include an item merely because information exists about it.
Include it in the executive review only if it:
- threatens an enterprise commitment
- creates material security/regulatory/customer exposure
- requires a cross-functional decision
- represents a reusable platform gap
- reveals a systemic operating problem

15. Never obscure uncertainty with confident language.
The purpose of the system is to improve decision quality, not make incomplete information appear certain.
